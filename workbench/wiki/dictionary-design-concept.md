# Design concept: the shared understanding of what the project is

A design concept is what the people and agents working on a project understand
the project to be, held in common between them. It is what alignment produces
and what delivery serves, in the owner's own definition. The
canonical definition is the [glossary entry](../../GLOSSARY.md#destination-and-direction).

**What it means here.** It exists between participants. `BLUEPRINT.md` helps them reconstruct it but is not itself the design concept. A document can record parts of it, and
every durable owner helps people recover it, but the concept itself lives in
the shared understanding, which is why it can drift when the people stop
talking and the documents stop being read.

**Neighbouring words.** [Align](dictionary-align.md) is the inquiry in which an
[Idea](dictionary-idea.md) becomes a design concept, and
[Confirm](dictionary-confirm.md) is the owner's agreement that the readback
names it correctly; only then is it mapped, planned or implemented. A
[Destination Question Card](dictionary-destination-question-card.md) keeps the
structured trail of how one part of it was reached, and the
[Landmark Wiki page](dictionary-landmark-wiki-page.md) keeps a readable
account. A [Prototype](dictionary-prototype.md) can sharpen it without writing
anything durable.

**In use.** "Is this still the design concept?" is the question a reviewer asks
when a Spec's acceptance has drifted from what the owner confirmed. The
Workbench's own design concept is reconstructed from the four-part Blueprint,
the decision records and the Wiki, never from one of them alone.
