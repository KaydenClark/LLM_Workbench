# Prototype: a rough version that answers what words cannot

Prototype is building a rough version of something to answer a question that
discussion cannot settle. It is optional; it needs no map and lands nothing in
enduring context. The canonical definition is the
[glossary entry](../../GLOSSARY.md#workflow-verbs).

**What it means here.** It comes before Confirm or between Confirm and Plan ([the prototype decision](../docs/ddr/000D-prototype-needs-no-map-and-lands-nothing-in-enduring-context.md)). The `prototype` skill carries the method. What it produces is evidence for a
decision, not the decision: the answer it finds is confirmed and then mapped
like any other.

**Neighbouring words.** It sits beside [Align](dictionary-align.md) and
[Confirm](dictionary-confirm.md), before [Plan](dictionary-plan.md). It has no
plane assigned ([Writer verb](dictionary-writer-verb.md)), because it writes
nothing durable. It is not [Implement](dictionary-implement.md): a prototype is
thrown away once it has answered its question.

**In use.** For example, "prototype the board layout before we confirm it" means: build a
throwaway page, look at it together, and confirm the layout that works; the
throwaway page is not merged and no Spec is written for it.
